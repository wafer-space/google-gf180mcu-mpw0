What was built

The run as a whole: what its designs add up to.

One of the free open-source GF180MCU shuttles that Google sponsored and Efabless ran. Each design sat in the user area of the Caravel harness, which took 55% of every die for its management SoC, pads and wiring. Its designs are measured here the same way as wafer.space's own runs, so you can compare them with a wafer.space die, where everything inside the pad ring is your design. The other Google shuttle measured here is MPW-18H1.

How people used their silicon

Where the area of 40 dies (805 mm² of silicon) actually went

40%
55%
  • Logic cells 1% — gates and flip-flops doing the design's actual work
  • Filler and tap cells 4% — inserted by the tools to keep rows continuous and wells tied
  • Other 40% — routing channels, analog and custom layout, power grid and empty space
  • Outside the user area 55% — the Caravel harness around the user's design: its management SoC, pads and wiring

Data and method

Everything on this page is measured from the public layout; the dates come from the Efabless GFMPW-0 shuttle page (archived) and the shuttle repositories.

  • Logic cells are standard cell instances, excluding fillers, taps, antenna diodes and tie cells.
  • Transistors are places where gate polysilicon crosses diffusion, not counting dummy fill.
  • Core area is the area of each die's user_project_wrapper cell.
  • Excluded from the measurements: pad ring (gf180mcu_*_io__*); caravel harness (caravel_core, caravel_power_routing, chip_io, caravel_logo, caravel_motto, copyright_block, open_source, user_id_textblock); seal ring (sealring). Die sizes are measured on the whole die, and bond pads on the whole die apart from its seal ring.
  • Bond pads are the pad openings in the pad ring, or in a second ring of pads inside it for chip-on-board bonding; probe pads are the others, next to the blocks they test. Letters and logos on the pad layer are not pads.
  • Theoretical maximum tiles one cell at its placement boundary with no routing.
  • Densest mm² is the best 1 mm square anywhere in each die, sliding in 0.1 mm steps and avoiding SRAM.
